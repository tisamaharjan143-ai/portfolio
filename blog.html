Do we really feel fine?

For many of us “I am fine” is a phrase that rolls off our tongue with practiced ease whenever we're asked how we're doing. It has become a default answer because it's polite, safe and convenient. But behind those three words lies a heavy truth we often tend to hide.

Saying “I'm fine” spares us from explaining something we ourselves don't quite understand yet and it saves the other person from worrying. We live in a world where everything is presented to be perfect. So, when it comes to us, we feel uncomfortable to admit that were struggling thinking it'll put a negative impression.

Overtime, this habit builds a huge wall between what we feel and what we want others to know which later can make us lose touch with our emotions. And when the feelings are neglected for too long, they resurface all at once which causes a mental breakdown. That is when we realize pretending to be fine only makes things worse for us.

So, how do we free ourselves from this habit?

It doesn't have to be about talking to a psychiatrist or going to therapy. It can start simple like journaling every night. Writing our feelings down can help us untangle the mess in our mind. Whenever you're asked how you're feeling, ask yourself before answering right away. And if you're feeling tired, say it. Admitting your feelings is the best step you can take to untangle yourself from this.

At the end of the day, "I'm fine" is something easier to say. But easy doesn't always mean best or growth. To grow we must take a harder path. So, from now on delete those words from your dictionary and say what you truly feel out loud.
